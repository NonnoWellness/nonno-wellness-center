What sober living looks like at Nonno

Early recovery is fragile without the right environment. Our sober living homes give you structure, accountability, and a community of people doing the same work you are — without the isolation of going it alone.

House managers help you get connected to a sponsor, organize your weekly schedule, and stay accountable through regular drug testing and house meetings. It's structured, but it's also genuinely lived-in — residents describe it as feeling more like family than a facility.

Who it's for

  • Stepping down from residential treatment or detox and need a stable, sober environment
  • Rebuilding routine, accountability, and independence after active addiction
  • Wanting community and peer support rather than recovering in isolation
  • Men and women ready to commit to house structure and regular testing

What to expect

  1. Move-in & orientationYou're paired with a house manager and introduced to house expectations and your housemates.
  2. Sponsor & scheduleWe help you get connected with a sponsor and build a weekly recovery schedule right away.
  3. Daily structureRegular testing, house meetings, chores, and access to AA/NA meetings and peer support.
  4. IndependenceAs you stabilize, you take on more responsibility — some residents become house managers themselves.

FAQs

Is sober living the same as treatment?

Not exactly — sober living provides housing, structure, and accountability, while IOP provides clinical treatment. Many residents do both at the same time.

How long do people typically stay?

It varies, but many residents stay 3–9 months while they rebuild routine and independence. There's no fixed program length — we work with where you're at.

Do you test regularly?

Yes. Consistent testing is part of what keeps the house environment safe and accountable for everyone living there.
