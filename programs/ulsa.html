Why ULSA exists

Union tradesmen, longshoremen, and dockworkers face a specific version of the same problem everyone in recovery faces: needing real treatment without losing the job, the union standing, or the trust of the crew that took years to build. ULSA (Union Lifestyle Substance Abuse and Mental Health) was built around that exact tension.

We coordinate care around job-site and port schedules, with peer support from others in the trades — including longshoremen and dockworkers — who understand the specific pressures of the work: physical demands, unpredictable shift hours, and a culture that doesn't always make it easy to ask for help.

Who it's for

  • Union members, tradesmen, ILWU longshoremen, and dockworkers worried treatment will cost them their job or standing
  • Anyone needing care that works around unpredictable job-site or port shift hours
  • People who want peer support from others who understand the trades and port work
  • Members looking for confidential, coordinated care through their union benefits

FAQs

Will my union or employer be notified?

We handle care confidentially and coordinate only what's necessary through your union benefits. Call us to talk through exactly what that looks like for your situation.

Can treatment work around unpredictable job-site hours?

Yes — that's the core of what ULSA is built for. We coordinate scheduling around your actual work commitments rather than a fixed clinic schedule.

Is ULSA only for certain trades?

No — ULSA is open to union members across trades, including ILWU longshoremen, dockworkers, and other port employees. Call us and we'll talk through whether it's the right fit for your situation.

Do you work with longshoremen and port workers?

Yes. Port shift schedules can be unpredictable, and we build treatment around that rather than asking you to work around a fixed clinic schedule. We also now operate a sober living home in San Pedro exclusively for ILWU members — call us to talk through availability.
